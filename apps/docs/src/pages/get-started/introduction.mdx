Jali is a React library for apps where people work on several things at once. Each thing gets its own pane, and the panes sit in rows, columns and tabs. You describe the kinds of pane your app has. Jali decides where they go, how they fit the window, and how people move between them.

## The problem

Some work doesn't happen one screen at a time. A support agent reads a ticket while checking the customer's last order. A credit reviewer keeps an application open beside the borrower's file. Someone in operations compares two shipments side by side.

Most web apps show one view at a time, so people open browser tabs, lose their place and copy details between windows. Building a workspace of panes by hand is a lot of work. You have to split and resize, decide what happens when the window gets narrow, keep focus in the right place, and remember the layout.

Jali is that work, done once, for any screen size.

## What it gives you

### Panes that fit instead of being crushed

Each [pane kind](/get-started/key-concepts) says the narrowest and shortest it can be and still work. When the window is too small for every pane, Jali folds some of them into tabs of their neighbours, instead of squeezing all of them until none is usable. When the room comes back, so do the panes.

The pane you are working in, the pane it was opened from, and the details it opened are the last to fold. On a phone, the workspace shows one pane at a time; widen the window again and the arrangement you had comes back.

### Focus, undo and the keyboard

One pane has focus, and commands act on it. Closing or tucking a pane away can be undone. <kbd className="kbd">F6</kbd> moves focus to the next pane, and <kbd className="kbd">⌘K</kbd> (<kbd className="kbd">Ctrl+K</kbd> on Windows and Linux) opens a command palette. You can change or turn off every key.

A pane can report unsaved work, such as an edited query that hasn't run. Jali asks before any change would lose it.

### A detail that follows a list

Open a record from a list, and the record's pane follows the list's selection: move down the list and the detail shows each row in turn. It waits until the selection rests, so holding a key doesn't load every row on the way, and Back skips the rows passed through. Pin the detail to keep it on one record.

### The layout in the address

The arrangement is written into the URL. A link opens the same panes in the same places, and Back and Forward step through the changes people made. Dragging a divider updates the address without adding to the history. If your app has a router, it can own the address instead.

### Curated pages

For work that centres on one item, such as reviewing a loan application, you can design a page: a set of views in tabs, with a list of items beside it.

- In **Single** mode, people see one view at a time, like any web app, and links open in a sheet over it.
- In **Multiplexed** mode, they can lay the views out side by side, starting from the layout you designed.

Records opened by following links go after the designed layout, and **Back to the page** clears them.

## Two packages

[`@jali/core`](/reference/core) holds the model: the layout tree, fitting, and a store with focus, undo and following. It doesn't depend on React or any other framework. [`@jali/react`](/reference/react) renders it, with components, hooks and a default theme.

## When not to use it

- **An app that shows one view at a time.** If people never need two things on screen together, a router and ordinary pages are simpler.
- **A dashboard of fixed tiles.** Jali is for panes that people open, close, move and resize. A grid of charts that never changes shape doesn't need a layout tree.
- **Windows that float and overlap.** Jali's panes tile: they sit in rows, columns and tabs, not on top of each other.
